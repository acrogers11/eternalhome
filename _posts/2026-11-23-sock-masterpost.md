![image](assets/images/socks/header.png)

i’ve been compiling my thoughts on socks for some time now, trying different kinds, keeping notes. tomato mountain may be the perfect venue to break down my findings. socks are the unsung heroes of our wardrobe, nuanced in design and material, ranging in price and quality. i’ve tried so many kinds over the years and have thinned the herd to only my favorites in recent years. these are the standouts, tested and approved.


![image](assets/images/socks/lifelong.png)

**life long by glen clyde**

**35 USD**

**nylon cordura yarn**

**made in japan**

*recommended for casual use but also fancy occasions, socks have a slight sheen to them*

the claim with these is that they’re tough *and* look nice. i have found both claims to be true. they show little pilling, light signs of wear, and have held up extremely well in the wash over the last few years. they actually look very fresh. they are a pleasing height for basically all occasions. i would feel comfortable with these in any casual situation. they feel nice on the foot, if a bit tight out of the wash, but they loosen up with a little movement. the fancy material lives up to its promise but it’s also the source of my criticism: these socks are not great for outdoor applications. i don’t like them for hiking or cycling very much. the material, which is basically plastic, gets stinky and isn’t nearly as comfortable as natural fibers. i think there’s a clear takeaway here about technology, and about the beauty of impermanent things. these socks, while beautiful, fill a narrow niche in my life. they appeal to a utilitarian paradigm, but what's the fun in that? they make the claim that they are less wasteful to buy and more environmentally friendly because they won’t be thrown out anytime soon. of course this claim doesn’t factor in the microplastics they leave in the environment and shed while washed. a sheep’s wool must be shorn anyway, so what is really worse? they do look nice on my legs though. 


![image](assets/images/socks/mag.png)

**maggie’s organic**

varies, but between **12 and 30 USD**

**wool, cotton, nylon, spandex**

**made in USA**

*recommended for daily use*

i’ve tried the ragg, wool, lightweight, mountain hiker, and organic cotton crew socks from maggies and they are awesome. the cotton crew socks are my most worn variety. i have a lot of pairs because they’re sold in three-packs and they’re what i ask for on gifty occasions. they never feel too tight or too loose. they make no special claims other than the fact that they’re made of organic cotton, which i think is great. though it’s ambiguous to me where the cotton is grown, it seems like it’s not grown domestically since they’re apparently fair trade certified while being “proudly knit and dyed in the USA.” obviously production cotton is really fried crop requiring a ton of water, but that’s another discussion. the black ones fade predictably after a number of washes which i don’t mind, and i’ve had a few develop small holes, always in the seems where the toe meets in the body of the sock, but nothing catastrophic yet. sarah wears her clothes a lot harder than me and hers have held up fine, better in fact than some more premium socks. the wool crews are very nice but they’re a bit thin for me, and don’t hold up well to repeated washings. the hiking socks are very nice quality but pricey. the ragg are fine, a bit too thin and more decorative than functional. and finally the lightweight version are good for a time and place, but sarah gets a lot more use out of them than me. i find my feet sliding around in my shoes with those, generally preferring something a little bit more robust.


![image](assets/images/socks/inj.png)

**injinji**

**18 USD**

**nylon, coolmax, lycra** 

**made in china**

*recommended for biking, hiking*

moving right along, injinji’s thing is toe socks. my sister put me on these because she’s a big foot health person. the idea being that your overall foot health is superior when you have increased mobility of the toes or something along those lines. sarah also really loves to rock these with bedrock sandals on some superfreak shit. they work great hiking and riding the bike longer distances. i’m actually favoring these right now for wearing to sleep in the winter because they don’t get all sweaty and weird. they come in various colors. they're a little trickier to get on in the morning, and the only other drawback is the big ass logo on the top of the foot. why does the world need to know what brand of outdoors socks you’re wearing? are they individual toes not enough of a tell?


![image](assets/images/socks/methok.png)

**methok pema of tibet**

**12 USD** (wow)

**cashmere and merino wool**

**made in tibet**

*recommended for cozy home use, biking, light hiking*

these are magical, sold by a magical person, but cumbersome to get ahold of. they are however maybe the most luxurious socks on the list. they’re medium to heavy weight yarn, merino cashmere blend. they hug your foot, feel as warm and cozy as any other wool garment i rock, and possess an apparent durability. i’ve worn them to work quite a bit and they’re barely pilling. to top it all off they’re very fairly priced. they come in a ton of great colors too. the only caveat is that they seem to only be available at the alameda flea market, sold at a booth near the front by a delightful and warm person. the storefront from which they used to be sold at is no more, so be prepared with cash at the flea. they are well-worth the hassle. if i could only have one style in my lineup, it would be these.


![image](assets/images/socks/nodal.png)

**nodal**

**28 to 36 USD**

**acrylic, wool, polyester, polyurethane, cotton, nylon, etc**

**made in japan**

*recommended for cozy relaxing at home, flexing on friends, some outdoorsy things, general use*

sarah and i discovered these fancy tabi socks at an REI type department store during our trip to japan several years ago. these are luxurious socks and the price reflects it. they make several styles ranging from super lightweight to thick, double faced and brushed pile. the colors are also very good. the durability is questionable as a few of our pairs have had some stitching on the toe pop, but that also might be a testament to how much sarah wears them. it should be noted too that they look damn good. they used to be a real pain to proxy into the states but looks like now they have a US distribution system. it seems like this comes at a pretty steep upcharge however. i guess that’s just the way everything’s going these days!
